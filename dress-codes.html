⛓️ DRESS CODES / KINK

New to gay nightlife, fetish gear or cruising? Here's what the terms on venue and event listings usually mean, in plain language.

Descriptions are a starting point, not a guarantee of admission or activities. Always check the individual venue or organizer's latest rules.

Dress codes & gear

Underwear

Underwear nights usually mean arriving or changing into briefs, boxers, trunks or similar underwear. Some venues specify what counts.

Jockstrap

A jockstrap is underwear with a front pouch and straps that leave the buttocks uncovered. Sometimes a required or encouraged outfit.

Naked / Nude

Events marked naked generally expect guests to be nude in designated areas. Rules about shoes, towels and where to undress vary.

Leather

Leather clothing and accessories such as trousers, jackets, boots, chaps or harnesses. Leather nights may require substantial gear rather than one small item.

Chaps

Leg coverings, often leather, that leave the crotch and seat uncovered. Worn over underwear or as part of fetish gear.

Rubber / Latex

Close-fitting, shiny latex garments such as suits, shorts and tops. Some events require genuine latex or rubber rather than lookalike materials.

Glossy synthetic material that can resemble latex visually but is not the same material. Check whether the event accepts PVC.

Neoprene

A thicker, flexible, often wetsuit-like material used in fetish and sports-inspired clothing.

Harness

Straps worn around the torso, commonly leather, rubber or fabric. A harness alone may not meet a strict fetish dress code.

Sportswear

Athletic outfits such as football kits, tracksuits, running shorts and jerseys. Some parties specify team kit or particular fabrics.

Sneakers / Trainers

Sports shoes; sneaker-themed events may prefer recognizable trainers paired with other athletic clothes.

Boots / Workwear

Heavy boots, industrial clothing, work uniforms and practical gear. Specific parties may request certain styles.

Uniform

Police, military, security or other occupational-inspired costumes or gear, usually as part of role-play aesthetics.

Dress code: Strict

An enforced entry rule. If the event says strict leather, rubber, sportswear or naked, you may be refused entry without the correct clothing.

Dress code: Encouraged

A suggested look or theme rather than a guaranteed entry requirement. The organizer's current rules always take precedence.

Spaces & nightlife terms

Cruising

Meeting and flirting with people in spaces associated with sexual encounters. Cruising does not mean anyone has consented to being approached or touched.

Darkroom

A dimly lit or unlit adult area within some venues. Availability, layout and etiquette vary by venue and by event.

Backroom / Play area

A designated adults-only part of a venue intended for intimate encounters, subject to venue rules and individual consent.

Sex club / Cruise bar

A nightlife venue that prioritizes encounters and cruising. A cruise bar may still have ordinary bar and social areas.

Public cruising area

An outdoor or semi-public location such as a park or beach that has a reputation for cruising. Local laws and conditions apply.

Fetish party

An event centered around particular outfits, interests, communities or sexual activities. Not every fetish party has the same facilities.

Men Only

An admission policy specified by the organizer. Read the current event's entry conditions rather than assuming all nights have identical rules.

Mixed / Queer night

An event welcoming a broader mix of genders and communities. Access policies, facilities and atmosphere may differ by night.

Bear / Mature / Young Crowd

Community or crowd descriptors, not strict promises about attendees. 'Bear' often refers to a larger or hairier masculine style.

Twink

A commonly used term for a slim, youthful-looking adult man. It describes a look, not an event's admission policy.

Kink & interaction

Kink / Fetish

Kink is an umbrella term for non-conventional erotic interests; a fetish is a strong focus on a particular item, material, body part or scenario.

BDSM

A broad umbrella for consensual bondage, discipline, dominance, submission and related practices. The activities and boundaries vary widely.

A dominant person enjoys taking the leading role; a submissive person enjoys giving up some control within agreed limits. A switch enjoys either role.

Consensual restriction of movement using items such as cuffs, rope or straps. Experience, circulation and the ability to stop matter.

A consensual role-play community involving puppy-inspired behavior and often hoods, collars or other gear. It does not imply any particular sexual activity.

Role-play

Consensually taking on agreed characters, identities or power dynamics. Anyone can set limits or stop at any point.

Foot Fetish

Erotic interest in feet, shoes, socks or related activities; not automatically part of every gear or sneaker event.

Body Worship

An erotic focus on appreciating or touching a partner's body, only within the boundaries both people agree to.

Vanilla

An informal term for sexual preferences that do not emphasize particular kinks; it is not a judgment or value ranking.

Safe Word / Stop Signal

Aftercare

Consent, boundaries & practicalities

Consent is always individual and ongoing. A venue, dress code, outfit or darkroom is never permission to touch someone. Respect a “no”, a refusal to engage or someone moving away. Ask before touching, photographing or involving anybody in an activity.

Know the rules. Entry policies, clothing checks, cameras, phones, footwear, smoking, hygiene and the availability of play spaces can change between nights at the same venue. Check the official event details before travelling.

Take care of yourself and others. Agree on limits before activities, be able to stop, take breaks, consider safer-sex supplies and seek help from staff when something feels unsafe. Public cruising areas may have different legal and personal-safety considerations from private clubs.
