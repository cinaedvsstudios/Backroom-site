👣First Time Cruising — What to Expect

You don't have to arrive with a plan. Some guys walk around, exchange looks and pause when someone catches their eye. Others hang back, check out the atmosphere or decide it isn't their night. All of that is normal.

You might find a busy location one evening and a quiet one the next. Give yourself time to get comfortable, and don't feel you have to join in just because you've turned up. If you want to leave, leave.

🗺️Different Places, Different Experiences

Outdoor places such as parks or beaches are usually less predictable: activity depends on the time, weather and who happens to be there. Public toilets are shared facilities, so respect other people using them. Local rules and laws around public sexual activity can be different from one city or country to another.

Indoor cruising clubs, saunas and darkrooms generally have their own entry conditions, opening hours, dress codes and facilities. Check the individual Backroom listing before heading out, especially when you're travelling. A listed cruising spot isn't a guarantee that anyone will be there.

👀Consent & Reading the Signals

A “no” means no, regardless of the location or situation. This also includes nonverbal rejection: if you are standing watching someone and they wave you away, you are expected to move on and look for someone else.

Eye contact and body language usually communicate interest more effectively than words. In fact, in a cruising area such as a park or a darkroom, it can feel a bit strange to start having a conversation with someone.

The key to good cruising is eye contact. Similarly, the key to rejecting someone clearly is a lack of eye contact. If you are staring someone down and they are looking everywhere except at you, they are not interested.

A touch on the arm or shoulder should be more than enough to indicate interest. If the person does not respond, that means they are not interested and you should move on.

🛑Know Your Boundaries

Know what you want or, more importantly, what you do not want. Negotiating this nonverbally can be something as simple as moving someone’s hand away from your arse.

🎒Before You Go — Practical Essentials

Check the location and opening times, think about how you'll get home and see whether the venue has any entry rules or cash-only payments. Bring a charged phone, whatever personal essentials you prefer, and any medication you need. Water and lubricant can be useful depending on your plans.

Take only what you need, particularly at outdoor spots. Above all, remember that you can change your plans at any time — cruising should be something you enjoy, not something you feel obliged to do.

💊PrEP, PEP & HIV Prevention

PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) is medication taken by people without HIV to prevent HIV. When taken correctly, it's highly effective at preventing HIV from sex. For guys who cruise regularly, or who simply want the reassurance of protection, it's worth knowing about.

Daily PrEP tablets are a common option. Some gay and bisexual men can also take on-demand PrEP (sometimes called “2-1-1”) around planned sex. It needs the right medication and timing, isn't suitable for everyone, and availability and recommendations vary between countries. Some places also offer longer-acting PrEP injections. A sexual health clinic can help you choose what fits your circumstances.

Before starting PrEP, you'll need an HIV test, and regular follow-up appointments include HIV screening and, for certain tablets, kidney checks. Routine STI testing is also part of looking after your sexual health. PrEP prevents HIV, but not other STIs.

PEP (post-exposure prophylaxis) is different: it's emergency medication after a possible HIV exposure. If you think you might need it, seek medical advice as soon as possible — ideally within 24 hours and no later than 72 hours. And one important fact worth knowing: U=U means that people living with HIV who maintain an undetectable viral load on treatment do not transmit HIV through sex.

Want the details? Read NHS guidance on daily and on-demand PrEP, WHO guidance on 2-1-1, or CDC guidance on PEP. Local clinics can explain what is available where you live.

🤫Privacy & Cruising Etiquette

Keep other people's privacy in mind. Don't photograph or film anyone without their permission, and don't share pictures or details that could identify someone else. A guy's interest in cruising doesn't mean he wants to be outed.

Respect people who aren't taking part, don't block paths or entrances, and leave a shared space as you found it. If someone isn't interested, don't follow or pressure them. Moving on gracefully is part of the experience.

🛡️Personal Safety

Stay aware of your surroundings, especially in outdoor areas after dark. Keep valuables secure and avoid carrying excessive cash or expensive items.

Trust your instincts. If something feels off, leave immediately.
